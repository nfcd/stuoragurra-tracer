      How to use

      Tracing

      • T Trace mode: click along a scarp to add points. Enter, Esc or double-click finishes the trace; the next click starts a new one. Backspace removes the last point while drawing.
      • Trace the foot or the crest consistently; NW-facing steps (NW side down) are the scarps.

      Editing a trace

      • V Select mode, then click a trace (or its name in the list). Its points appear; the green one is the start.
      • M Move: drag points. Drag (or click) the small round handles in the middle of segments to insert a point there.
      • I Insert: click on or near the trace; the point is inserted in the nearest segment. (Shift-click on a line also inserts.)
      • D Delete: click points to delete them. (Alt-click or right-click also deletes.)
      • A / C add points at the start / end of the selected trace; Enter when done.
      • Click a point to select it: edit its E / N, step with [ ], nudge with the arrow keys (Shift: 5 m), Del deletes it, Esc deselects it.
      • Cmd/Ctrl+Z undo, Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+Z redo.

      Showing and hiding traces

      • The ● / ○ button in the list shows or hides a trace. H hides the selected trace, Shift+H shows all. Only selected hides all others.
      • Hidden traces are kept and exported (with visible: false).

      Profiles

      • P Profile mode: click two points. The topography (1 m sampling) appears in the panel below; drag the end points to adjust. The panel is shown only in Profile mode (like the Review panel in Review mode); V or Esc leaves the mode and closes it.
      • Hover the chart to see the position on the map. Keep stores the profile; CSV downloads it.

      Reviewing profiles

      • R Review mode: profiles every 100 m along your traces, perpendicular to the local strike, named T07_P012 (trace 7, 12th profile from the trace's first point).
      • The chart shows the swath profile: median (black) and 25–75 % range (light blue) of the elevations within ± the swath width along strike; the thin blue line is the single central profile; the red dashed line is the trace. Half-length and swath width can be changed; they are stored with the export. Vertical exaggeration sets the chart scale as in the Profile panel: 1× (true scale, the default) to 5×, or auto (fit to the panel, factor shown); with a fixed factor the axes extend so that the whole profile fits.
      • Profiles (toolbar): draws the review profiles on the map in every mode, all or only the accepted ones, coloured by decision; click one to open it in Review mode.
      • Show (Review panel): only the accepted, unsure, rejected or not-reviewed profiles are drawn, and ← / → step through them only.
      • Y accept, N reject, U unsure, 0 clear; each decision moves to the next profile. ← / → previous / next, Space next unreviewed. Click a profile on the map to go to it.
      • The numbers under the buttons are advice only: local step height (lines fitted 10–30 m either side) and how planar the ground is 10–60 m either side (RMS scatter).
      • Decisions are saved in this browser; Export review writes them as CSV. If a trace is edited, decisions for profiles that moved more than 5 m are shown as outdated.

      Saving

      • Everything is saved automatically in this browser (for this web address).
      • Paper's interpretation loads the scarp traces and the profile review used in the paper (Cardozo and Watson); the accepted profiles are those analysed there.
      • Export GeoJSON regularly to keep your traces as a file (ETRS89 / UTM 33N, EPSG:25833); Import traces loads such a file again. In Review mode, Export review / Import review do the same for the profile decisions (CSV).
      • New starts from scratch: it removes all traces, profiles and review decisions stored in this browser (export first to keep them).

      Map

      • Scarp index: elevation step across ±5 m perpendicular to the fault, minus the regional slope (0–3 m colour scale). Elevation: colours with hillshade. Data: Kartverket / høydedata.no.
      • 1 elevation, 2 hillshade, 3 texture shading, 4 scarp index.
      • Hillshade: grey shading with the sun from the NW (azimuth 315°, altitude 35°). Texture shading (fractional Laplacian, α = 0.8, wavelengths > ~300 m suppressed) shows fine relief while keeping the local shape of the terrain. Both have contrast / brightness sliders.
      • Elevation colours: set Min / Max in the side panel, or press F to stretch the colours to the area in view (2–98 % of elevations). A narrow range around a scarp makes small steps and their terminations stand out. The hillshade slider sets how much shading is mixed in (0 = colours only).
